# ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE USE POLICY
**[Library Name]**  
*Adopted by the Board of Trustees on [Date]*  
*Version 0.3.1, 2026*

## I. Introduction and Purpose
[Library Name] holds the trust of the public it serves and the funds the public provides. As artificial intelligence tools become increasingly available across staff workflows, public services, and vendor platforms, the library has a continuing stewardship responsibility to evaluate, authorize, oversee, and reconsider AI-enabled uses transparently and in ways that protect patron privacy, intellectual freedom, and equitable access, consistent with the [American Library Association's Guidance on the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Libraries](https://www.ala.org/tools/standards-and-guidelines/guidance-use-artificial-intelligence-libraries).

The purpose of this Policy is to establish clear, values-grounded decision rights, review criteria, participation routes, and accountability records for how [Library Name] governs AI-enabled uses across its operations and public services.

This Policy applies to all library trustees, staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors acting on behalf of [Library Name].

## II. Definitions
For the purposes of this Policy:
- **AI-Enabled Use (Unit of Review):** A particular feature, workflow, service, program, or decision context in which the library selects, configures, recommends, or materially relies on an artificial intelligence or automated decision-support function. **Material reliance** occurs when an output meaningfully shapes staff action, public content, patron access, ranking, resource allocation, or another decision, regardless of the vendor's technical or marketing label. Approval of a software product for one use does not authorize other features or subsequent vendor updates.
- **Authorized Disposition vs. Review State:** An **authorized disposition** states what activity may occur and under which conditions (permitted, restricted, limited internal exercise, deferred, refused, retired, or time-limited exception with controls). A **review state** (`Review required`, `Underway`, or `Complete`) describes evaluation activity only. Starting a review grants no permission, and an authorized internal staff exercise does not authorize a public pilot.
- **Closed AI System:** An AI tool or platform operating under enforceable contract terms and technical controls ensuring that inputs are not retained for model training, shared with unauthorized subprocessors, or accessible outside [Library Name]'s authorized account.
- **Open AI System:** An AI tool or platform where input data may be retained, used to train public or commercial models, or accessed by third parties, or where vendor terms are unverified.
- **Patron Data:** Any information that identifies or could reasonably identify a patron, including contact details, circulation history, reading records, search queries, conversational catalog prompts, reference questions, event attendance, or digital activity.
- **Vendor-Added AI:** AI features integrated or updated within software, discovery layers, catalog systems (ILS), or subscription databases supplied by external vendors.

## III. Core Principles
All selection, evaluation, authorization, and reconsideration of AI-enabled uses at [Library Name] shall adhere to six core principles:

1. **Patron Privacy is Paramount.** What patrons read, search, ask, or access is confidential. No patron data shall be entered into an open AI system under any circumstances, and unresolved live-query vendor terms prevent public activation.
2. **Intellectual Freedom and Unbiased Access.** AI features used in reference, discovery, cataloging, or collection workflows shall not silently restrict, filter, personalize, or bias access to information, and non-AI service alternatives must remain accessible.
3. **Human Responsibility and Delegated Judgment.** AI outputs are draft or decision-support assistance, not autonomous authorities. Staff remain accountable for all published content, purchasing decisions, and service outcomes.
4. **Proportionate Community and Staff Participation.** Community knowledge and staff experience inform problem definition, non-AI alternatives, evaluation criteria, and reconsideration in forms proportionate to the consequences of the decision.
5. **Transparency and Recourse.** The library will explain its AI decisions, limitations, and uncertainties openly and maintain clear routes for patrons and staff to correct errors, request human assistance, or reopen review.
6. **Proportional Governance and Environmental/Labor Stewardship.** Governance processes scale to local capacity while accounting for staff workload, supply chain labor standards, and environmental footprints.

## IV. Permitted, Restricted, and Prohibited Uses

### A. Permitted Routine Internal Uses (Grouped Core Record)
Staff may undertake comparable low-risk internal uses only within the scope and conditions of an **authorized disposition** recorded in the registry. These uses may share a **grouped core record** when their purpose, data, owner, conditions, and review arrangements are the same. Examples include:
- Drafting, editing, or brainstorming administrative text, grant applications, or promotional copy that contains **no** patron data or confidential personnel information.
- Creating administrative templates using aggregate or de-identified data.
- Using specifically reviewed and authorized low-risk embedded features (such as standard spell-check or grammar assistance) within their approved scope.

### B. Uses Requiring Individual Expanded Review and Explicit Authorization
The following uses trigger an **Expanded Impact Review** using [`AI_Tool_Evaluation_Checklist.md`](AI_Tool_Evaluation_Checklist.md) and require an explicit **Authorized Disposition** in [`AI_Tool_Registry_Template.md`](AI_Tool_Registry_Template.md) before activation:
- Processing any patron data, search queries, or reference interactions in a closed AI system.
- Activating or relying on optional, newly added, or updated AI features in the ILS, discovery catalog, website, or subscription databases.
- Moving from a bounded internal staff exercise (using invented queries) to a live patron pilot or public deployment.
- Publishing AI-assisted content externally in the library's name or materially relying on AI outputs to shape patron access, ranking, or resource allocation.
- Using AI tools to assist with selecting, weeding, classifying, or ranking collection materials.
- Purchasing, subscribing to, or renewing a platform with embedded AI functions.

### C. Prohibited Uses (Never Permitted)
The following are strictly prohibited:
- Entering confidential patron records, borrowing or search history, or identifiable reference questions into any open or unapproved AI tool.
- Entering personally identifiable information about any patron, especially a minor, into an open AI system.
- Using AI to screen, rank, or evaluate candidates for employment or volunteer roles.
- Using AI to profile, score, or predict the behavior of identifiable patrons.
- Using AI to generate official library policy positions or automated collection decisions without authorized human review.
- Using AI to create misleading, deceptive, or fabricated content.

## V. Patron Privacy and Confidentiality
1. **No Patron Data in Open AI Tools:** Patron data, search history, borrowing records, and reference queries shall never be entered into open AI tools.
2. **Vendor Assessment and Silence Rule:** Before enabling vendor-added AI that interacts with patron queries or data, the library must verify enforceable terms governing retention, model training, subprocessors, and local controls. Vendor nonresponse by a local deadline remains unresolved evidence and never expands permission.
3. **De-identification:** Aggregate, anonymized usage statistics may be processed using closed AI tools only when individual identity cannot be reestablished.

## VI. Participation in Defining and Reconsidering Decisions
1. **Complementary Knowledge:** Staff experience regarding service encounters and working conditions and community knowledge regarding local priorities and access needs are complementary; staff consultation does not substitute for community participation.
2. **Proportionate Depth:** Consequential or patron-facing applications require accessible opportunities for community input on the service problem, non-AI alternatives, and evaluation criteria before a feature is activated for public use, with outreach beyond frequent borrowers and enthusiastic adopters. Where local authority permits, specific evaluation criteria may require joint agreement with staff and community advisers, while formal approval and contracting remain with the designated authority.
3. **Routine Internal Uses and Recorded Gaps:** Routine internal uses may rely on documented prior community engagement and standing policy; if no community input informed the applicable policy, the registry record must state that gap explicitly rather than describing participation as inherited.
4. **Professional Obligations:** Participation operates within the library's legal and professional obligations; majority preference cannot authorize restrictions on other people's lawful access to materials.

## VII. Governance, Authority Map, and Registry

### A. Authority Functions
Rather than prescribing new job titles, the library assigns six stewardship functions (one person may hold several functions, and the record must identify where local, board, parent agency, or consortial authority actually resides):
- **Service Owner:** Oversees the specific AI-enabled use and routes concerns.
- **Reviewer:** Evaluates technical, collection, privacy, and participation evidence across Accuracy, Autonomy, Recourse, and Enforceability.
- **Approver:** Determines the authorized disposition within delegated powers.
- **Registry Maintainer:** Updates the decision record and change log.
- **Records Custodian:** Manages record access and retention under public records rules.
- **Authorized Risk Acceptor:** Approves any permitted time-limited exception within delegated authority.

### B. AI Tool & Use Registry
The library shall maintain an **AI Tool & Use Registry** ([`AI_Tool_Registry_Template.md`](AI_Tool_Registry_Template.md)) preserving for each in-scope use or grouped low-risk entry:
- Authorized name, purpose, alternatives considered, and data involved.
- Assigned authority functions and the separate **authorized disposition** and **review state**.
- Participation basis, recorded community/staff contributions, disagreements, and missing perspectives.
- Supporting evidence, unanswered questions, the specific activity prevented by each material uncertainty, and workload allocation.
- Conditions of use, immediate stop/escalation triggers, recourse routes, next review point, and change history.

### C. Essential or Imposed Platforms (Time-Limited Exceptions)
Where an essential or externally imposed vendor platform introduces an AI feature and immediate withdrawal is infeasible, the appropriate authority may record a **time-limited exception** only with a named **authorized risk acceptor**, specific compensating controls, an expiration date, and an escalation or exit route. An exception cannot waive legal or policy requirements beyond that actor's authority and never converts missing evidence into permanent approval.

### D. Reconsideration and Board Reporting
A vendor update outside approved scope or a recurring patron/staff concern reopens review; if an unapproved change cannot be isolated, the affected feature pauses pending a new decision. The director shall report at least annually to the Board of Trustees on active registry dispositions, exceptions, community participation outcomes, and any privacy or access concerns.

## VIII. Public Services and AI Literacy
1. **Public AI Access and Explanation:** When an AI-enabled feature is authorized for public use, the library will provide an accessible public explanation of its purpose, limitations, non-AI alternatives, and correction route.
2. **AI Literacy vs. Governance Participation:** The library will offer instruction and reference support to build critical AI literacy while distinguishing educational workshop attendance from governance participation that influences institutional choices.

## IX. Vendor Relations and Procurement
1. **Procurement Standards:** RFPs, contracts, and renewals shall require vendors to disclose embedded AI functions, prohibit model training on patron data, provide advance change notices, and preserve local configuration toggles.
2. **Default-Off Preference:** Optional or newly added vendor AI features must remain disabled until reviewed and explicitly authorized in the registry.

## X. Staged Implementation and Staff Guidance
1. **Staged Implementation Cycle:** Rather than a fixed calendar countdown, implementation follows four stages with explicit completion conditions: (1) *Establish scope and authority*, (2) *Develop evidence and participation*, (3) *Decide and communicate*, and (4) *Examine feasibility* within agreed workload allocations.
2. **Staff Guidance:** Every authorized disposition shall be translated into actionable staff instructions and summarized in [`AI_Quick_Reference_OnePager.md`](AI_Quick_Reference_OnePager.md).

## XI. Policy Review and Effective Date
This Policy takes effect immediately upon adoption by the Board of Trustees and shall be reviewed at least annually or whenever major technological, vendor, or legal changes warrant reconsideration.

## APPENDIX B: COMMON AI USES AND CLASSIFICATION

The following is provided for general reference. AI tools and vendor data practices change frequently; always verify current terms and local configuration controls before recording an authorized disposition.

**Typically Closed (Enterprise/Paid Tiers — verify contract terms):**
- ChatGPT Team/Enterprise (OpenAI), Microsoft 365 Copilot, Google Workspace Enterprise Gemini, and Claude Team/Enterprise (Anthropic) when configured under organizational tenant agreements that exclude model training.

**Typically Open (Free/Consumer Tiers — no patron data permitted):**
- Free consumer tiers of ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude, and consumer writing or image generators where inputs may be retained or used to improve services.

**Routine Embedded Low-Risk Uses (Eligible for Grouped Core Record):**
- Standard spell-check, grammar suggestions, and basic autocomplete in approved office software with no patron data exposure.

**Vendor-Added AI in Library Systems (Requires Individual Expanded Review):**
- Conversational catalog discovery, AI readers' advisory, search summarization, or automated ranking added to discovery layers, ILS platforms, or databases. Treat these as consequential Section IV.B uses requiring individual review, participation, and explicit disposition—never as automatic conveniences.
