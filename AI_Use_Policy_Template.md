# ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE USE POLICY

**[Library Name]**

*Adopted by the Board of Trustees on [Date]*
*Version 1.0, 2026*

## I. Introduction and Purpose

[Library Name] holds the trust of the public it serves and the funds the public provides. As artificial intelligence tools become increasingly available and capable, the library has both an opportunity and a responsibility to use them thoughtfully, transparently, and in ways that protect patron privacy, intellectual freedom, and equitable access.

This Artificial Intelligence Use Policy ("Policy") provides guidance for the responsible use of AI tools in the operations and services of [Library Name]. It is grounded in the profession's long-standing commitments: the Library Bill of Rights, the ALA Code of Ethics, the Freedom to Read, and the confidentiality of what patrons read, ask, and borrow. It is consistent with the American Library Association's Guidance on the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Libraries (2026), which names sustainability and labor among the values that govern AI use alongside privacy, intellectual freedom, and equitable access.

This Policy covers everyone who acts on the library's behalf: the director, managers, paid staff, and volunteers serving in any official capacity. It also covers anyone working with library-owned devices, accounts, or data. Governance structures vary; in this Policy, the library's governing authority (typically the Board of Trustees, with the director responsible for day-to-day administration) holds oversight. Adapt the authority language to your structure if the library is a municipal department or part of a larger system.

## II. Definitions

"Artificial intelligence" or "AI" refers to software that produces text, images, audio, summaries, recommendations, or rankings by recognizing patterns learned from large amounts of data. Familiar examples include ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Grammarly, and the AI features appearing inside software and databases the library already uses.

"AI-generated content" means any text, image, audio, video, or other material produced in whole or in part by an AI tool, including content drafted by AI and subsequently edited by a person.

"Closed AI system" describes a tool that keeps what you type private. Your entries are not used to train the underlying model and are walled off from other users. Paid and enterprise tiers commonly work this way, which makes them the safer choice for library work involving any non-public information.

"Open AI system" describes a tool that may learn from what you type. Your entries can be absorbed into the model's training data, and nothing guarantees they stay private. Most free consumer AI tools fall into this category.

"Embedded AI tools" are the small AI conveniences inside software the library has already adopted: spell-check and grammar suggestions, smart compose in email, automatic categorization in administrative software. Because the library has already vetted these products, their built-in conveniences need no separate sign-off under this Policy. New AI features added to existing platforms are a different matter; see Section VII.

"Patron data" means any information about library users, including but not limited to: names, contact information, borrowing and circulation records, holds and reading history, search and browsing history, reference questions, computer and Wi-Fi session logs, program registrations, and fines or fee records.

"Confidential patron records" means any record that connects an identifiable person to what they read, viewed, requested, or asked. In most states this confidentiality is protected by library-records law, and it is protected by the profession's ethics regardless of the jurisdiction.

## III. Professional Grounding and Guiding Principles

Libraries engage new technology with both openness and care. AI tools are neither inherently good nor inherently dangerous. They are instruments that reflect the values and intentions of those who use them. The following principles guide AI use at [Library Name]:

- **Stewardship:** The library is a steward, not an owner, of the public trust and public funds entrusted to it, including the time, data, and confidence of its patrons. AI tools should support the mission and extend staff capacity, not replace the human service relationships at the heart of library work.
- **Intellectual Freedom:** A tool that filters, ranks, recommends, or flags content is making access and collection decisions. The library will not let an AI tool quietly narrow what patrons can find or read. AI that shapes discovery or collection is reviewed against the Library Bill of Rights (Articles I and II on access to information, and Article V on the right to use the library) before it is deployed.
- **Patron Privacy and Confidentiality:** The confidentiality of what a patron reads, asks, and borrows is both a professional commitment and, in most jurisdictions, a legal protection (Library Bill of Rights, Article VII; ALA Code of Ethics, Principle III). AI tools must never be allowed to compromise it.
- **Equity of Access:** The library serves everyone. AI tools must not introduce bias or discrimination, and the library's own AI literacy work should help close, not widen, the gap between patrons who can navigate these tools and those who cannot.
- **Sustainability:** AI tools carry costs that never appear on the invoice: energy, water, hardware, and the strain of data centers on the communities that host them. When the library compares tools, documented lower environmental impact counts in a tool's favor, and a smaller tool that does the job well is preferred over a larger one that does the same job at greater cost.
- **Labor and Human Expertise:** AI should extend what staff can do, not erode what staff are for. The library will not use AI, or the efficiency it creates, to justify staff reductions, deskilling, or the replacement of professional judgment. When an AI tool would substantially change how people work, the people who do that work are consulted before the change is made.
- **Truth-Telling and Transparency:** The library will be transparent about when and how AI tools are used in public-facing communications and services. It will not represent AI-generated content as solely human-created work.
- **Discernment:** Not every possible use of AI is wise. Staff exercise judgment, individually and as an organization, about when AI tools serve patrons and when they do not.
- **Accountability:** The Board, as the governing authority, is responsible for oversight. Individual users are responsible for the content they produce and the data they handle, whether or not AI tools assisted.

## IV. Approved and Common Uses

AI tools may be used for the following purposes without prior Board approval, provided the user follows the guidelines in this Policy:

**Communications and Outreach**

- Drafting, editing, or brainstorming newsletter articles, announcements, social media posts, and website content.
- Generating initial drafts of letters, descriptions, or signage for subsequent human review and editing.
- Proofreading and improving the clarity of written communications.

**Programs, Instruction, and Readers' Advisory**

- Researching topics, background, and context for public programming and displays.
- Brainstorming program ideas, instructional outlines, or booklist themes.
- Generating initial drafts of program descriptions or handouts for staff review and revision.
- Note: see Section VI for transparency expectations on public-facing AI-assisted content, and for the intellectual-freedom cautions on AI-generated reading recommendations.

**Administration**

- Drafting meeting agendas, summarizing minutes, or organizing reports.
- Creating templates for budgets, schedules, or planning, using aggregate or de-identified figures.
- Using embedded AI features in already-approved library software.

## V. Prohibited Uses

The following uses of AI are prohibited at [Library Name] under all circumstances:

- **Confidential patron records in any AI system:** No record connecting an identifiable person to what they read, viewed, requested, borrowed, or asked may be entered into any AI tool, open or closed. This includes summarizing, paraphrasing, or anonymizing such records to seek analysis or advice. The confidentiality of patron use is the library's bedrock, and the risk of exposure makes any AI use here unacceptable.
- **Patron personal data in open AI systems:** No personally identifiable information about any patron, including names, contact information, or account details, may be entered into an open AI system.
- **Minors' data in any AI system:** No personally identifiable information about a minor, including names, photos, family information, or program participation records, may be entered into any AI tool, open or closed.
- **Personnel and HR decisions:** AI tools may not be used to evaluate, screen, rank, or make recommendations about candidates for employment or volunteer roles.
- **Generating official library positions:** AI tools may not be used to draft or generate official policy statements, collection development decisions, challenge or reconsideration responses, or any content that claims to represent the position of [Library Name] without thorough staff and Board review.
- **Generating content intended to deceive:** AI tools may not be used to create deepfakes, misleading images, impersonations, fabricated testimonials, or any content designed to misrepresent its origin or authenticity.
- **Surveillance of patron use:** AI tools may not be used to profile, score, or predict the behavior of identifiable patrons based on their library use.

## VI. Ethical Guidelines

Beyond what is prohibited, the following guidelines apply to all AI use at [Library Name]:

- **Transparency in Public-Facing Content:** When AI tools have played a substantial role in generating public-facing content, the responsible staff member should disclose it in a manner appropriate to the context. A brief note is sufficient. AI used only for research, spell-checking, or minor editing does not require disclosure.
- **Human Review Required:** All AI-generated content used in library communications, programs, instruction, or official business must be reviewed, edited, and approved by a responsible person before use. Treat AI output as a first draft; it is never ready to publish as it arrives. The person who publishes or presents the content answers for its accuracy, tone, and appropriateness.
- **Bias Awareness:** AI learns from the recorded past, and the recorded past carries prejudice. Generated content can quietly import assumptions about race, gender, culture, or class. Read AI output with a critical eye and correct anything that does not reflect the library's commitment to equitable, inclusive service.
- **Intellectual Freedom in Recommendation:** Be cautious with AI tools that recommend, rank, or filter reading and search results. A recommendation engine can narrow a patron's view without anyone choosing to narrow it. Where AI shapes what patrons see, preserve the patron's ability to browse the full collection and to reach a human for readers' advisory.
- **Intellectual Honesty:** If an AI tool did a substantial share of the drafting on a report, article, or instructional material, say so to the people receiving it. Presenting machine-drafted work as wholly your own misleads the community this Policy exists to serve.
- **No Substitute for the Service Relationship:** AI tools can support library work but cannot replace the reference interview, the readers' advisory conversation, instruction, or the welcoming human presence of the public service desk. AI should free up time for those, not replace them.

## VII. High-Risk Uses Requiring Board or Director Approval

The following uses carry elevated risk and require prior approval from the Board, the director, or a designated staff committee before implementation:

- **Patron data in closed AI systems:** Any use of personally identifiable patron data in a closed AI system (for example, analyzing circulation patterns or generating targeted communications) requires approval and a review of the tool's data handling and privacy practices.
- **New AI features in library systems:** When the ILS, discovery layer, public catalog, website, or a subscription database introduces new AI features that process patron data or shape search results, the library should review and approve those features before they are activated. This is the trigger libraries most often miss, because vendors add AI to platforms already in use.
- **AI-assisted content published in the library's name:** Any AI-assisted content published externally as the library's official voice, including press statements, grant applications, or formal communications, requires review by the director or designee.
- **AI in collection development:** The use of AI tools to select, weed, or rank materials for the collection requires review to protect against bias and against the quiet narrowing of access.
- **New AI tools or subscriptions:** Any purchase of, subscription to, or new account for an AI tool using library funds or for library purposes should be reported to and approved by the director or designated committee, and recorded in the library's AI tool registry.

## VIII. Data Stewardship

Patrons trust [Library Name] with information about what they read and ask. This section provides specific guidance for protecting that trust when AI tools are in use:

- **Default to closed systems:** When AI tools are used for any library purpose involving non-public information, use a closed AI system. If you are unsure whether a tool is open or closed, do not enter any library-related data until you have confirmed.
- **Minimize data input:** Enter only the minimum information necessary. Do not paste entire records, directories, or reports into AI tools when a summary or de-identified excerpt would do.
- **Review outputs for leakage:** Before sharing or publishing AI-generated content, review it to ensure it does not inadvertently reveal private information included in the prompt.
- **Vendor review:** Before any AI tool is used with patron data, review the tool's privacy policy and terms of service to understand how data is stored, used, retained, and shared. Record the finding in the AI tool registry.
- **Honor library-records law:** Treat patron records under your state's library confidentiality statute. In Texas, that is Government Code Section 552.124, which makes records identifying a patron's use of library materials or services confidential and exempt from public disclosure, with narrow exceptions (operational necessity, the patron's own right of access, and a law-enforcement court order or subpoena). Substitute your state's statute; the ALA maintains a state-by-state list. When in doubt, assume the record is protected.
- **Retention and deletion:** Many AI tools retain conversation history. Delete AI conversation histories that contain library-related information when the task is complete, where the tool allows it.

## IX. Oversight and Implementation

The Board is responsible for the oversight and enforcement of this Policy. The Board may direct the director, an existing committee, or a small working group to:

- Serve as the point of contact for questions about AI use at [Library Name].
- Maintain the AI tool registry and review high-risk AI uses as described in Section VII.
- Review new AI tools and vendor-added AI features for data handling and privacy practices.
- Recommend updates to this Policy as AI technology and professional guidance evolve.
- Report to the Board at least annually on the state of AI use at [Library Name].

This Policy should be reviewed and updated at least once per year, or more frequently as significant developments warrant.

## X. Training and Public Literacy

All paid staff will receive training on this Policy within 60 days of its adoption and as part of new employee orientation thereafter. Volunteers in public-facing or leadership roles will receive a summary and guidance relevant to their service.

Because the library's public role includes helping the community navigate new technology, the Board is encouraged to support AI literacy programming for patrons, consistent with [Library Name]'s commitment to equitable access and an informed public. This outward-facing literacy work is one of the six responsibilities of AI stewardship and is a distinctive strength of the public library.

## XI. Reporting Concerns

If you see AI being used in a way this Policy does not permit, or a use that simply concerns you, say something. Bring it to your supervisor, the director, or any trustee. The library will look into every concern raised in good faith, and no one at [Library Name] will face retaliation for raising one.

## APPENDIX A: QUICK REFERENCE GUIDE

This guide is a practical reference for staff and volunteers. When in doubt, ask before proceeding.

**You MAY use AI tools freely for:**

- Drafting and editing newsletters, social media posts, and announcements.
- Brainstorming program ideas, instructional outlines, or booklist themes.
- Researching topics, background, and context for programs and displays.
- Proofreading and grammar-checking written communications.
- Creating administrative templates using aggregate or de-identified data.
- Using embedded AI features in already-approved library software.

**You MUST ask the director (or designated committee) first before:**

- Using any patron data in a closed AI system.
- Activating new AI features in the ILS, catalog, website, or a database.
- Publishing AI-assisted content externally in the library's name.
- Using AI tools to select, weed, or rank materials for the collection.
- Purchasing or subscribing to a new AI tool for library use.

**You must NEVER:**

- Enter confidential patron records, borrowing or search history, or reference questions into any AI tool.
- Enter personally identifiable information about any patron, especially a minor, into an open AI system.
- Use AI to screen, rank, or evaluate candidates for employment or volunteer roles.
- Use AI to profile, score, or predict the behavior of identifiable patrons.
- Use AI to generate official library positions or collection decisions without staff and Board review.
- Use AI to create misleading, deceptive, or fabricated content.

**Always remember:**

- You are responsible for the content you produce, even when AI helped create it.
- AI output is a first draft, not a finished product. Review everything.
- When in doubt about whether a use is appropriate, ask your supervisor or the director.
- Be transparent about your use of AI. Honesty builds trust.

## APPENDIX B: COMMON AI TOOLS AND CLASSIFICATION

The following is provided for general reference. AI tools and their data practices change frequently. Last verified 2026; confirm current terms before relying on this table. The director or designated committee should verify current terms before approving any tool for use with patron data.

**Typically Closed (Paid/Enterprise Tiers):**

- ChatGPT Team/Enterprise (OpenAI): paid tiers do not use input for training by default.
- Microsoft 365 Copilot: operates within the organization's Microsoft 365 tenant.
- Google Workspace AI features (Gemini): enterprise tiers include data protection commitments.
- Claude Team/Enterprise (Anthropic): paid tiers do not use input for training.

**Typically Open (Free Tiers):**

- ChatGPT Free (OpenAI): inputs may be used for training unless opted out.
- Google Gemini (free consumer version): inputs may be used to improve services.
- Free AI writing tools, image generators, and chatbots: assume open unless verified otherwise.

**Embedded (Generally No Additional Approval Needed):**

- Spell-check and grammar suggestions in word processors or email.
- Smart compose and autocomplete in email clients.
- Auto-categorization in administrative software.

**Vendor-added AI in library systems (review before relying on it):**

- AI search, summarization, or "ask" features added to discovery layers, public catalogs, and subscription databases.
- AI features introduced into the ILS or website platform by the vendor.
- These often process patron queries or shape results. Treat new vendor AI as a Section VII review trigger, not an embedded convenience.

**Important:** Free-tier and open tools are acceptable for general research, brainstorming, and drafting where no patron data, confidential records, or reading history are involved. The key question is always: what information am I putting into this tool?
