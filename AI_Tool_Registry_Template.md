# AI Tool Registry

## A working template for public libraries

A registry is not a spreadsheet. A spreadsheet is a list that no one owns and no one maintains, and it fails the moment two branches disagree about what counts as the same tool. A registry is an authority record: it names a maintaining authority, it records where each entry came from, and it keeps the history of what changed and why. Libraries already do this work. You do it every time you maintain an authority file so that one author has one heading across a catalog held by many hands over many years. This template applies that same discipline to the AI-enabled tools moving through your library.

The goal is the smallest amount of structure that still lets you answer one question to a board, an auditor, or a patron: who approved this tool, on what terms, and when. If the registry cannot answer that, it is a spreadsheet.

---

## What the registry is for

It records the AI-enabled features in use across your library before scattered practice hardens into de facto policy. It does not approve tools by itself. It records the decision, the conditions, and the custody chain so that the decision can be reviewed, explained, and revisited.

Three library-specific stakes ride on this:

- **Intellectual freedom.** A tool that filters, ranks, or flags content is making collection and access decisions. The registry is where that gets named rather than absorbed silently.
- **Patron privacy.** Any tool that touches circulation data, search history, or patron records sits under your state's library-records confidentiality law. The registry records which tools touch patron data and under what handling terms.
- **Public accountability.** The registry itself may be a public record. Write every entry as though it could be read by the public it serves, because it may be.

---

## How a tool enters the registry

A tool enters through any of these triggers. Each trigger creates an entry; nothing reaches patrons without one.

- Procurement of a new platform or service
- Renewal of an existing contract
- A vendor adding an AI feature to a platform you already run
- A staff request to use a tool
- A pilot or trial
- A patron-facing deployment
- A reported concern about a tool already in use

The vendor-update trigger is the one libraries miss. Your discovery layer, your ILS, your databases will grow AI features you never procured. Renewal and the vendor-update trigger are how those enter the record instead of arriving unannounced.

---

## The entry

Each tool gets one entry. Keep the fields plain.

| Field | What it records |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Authorized name** | The single controlled name you will use for this tool everywhere. One tool, one heading. |
| **Also appears as** | Vendor names, marketing names, and the places it shows up embedded in other platforms. This is the cross-reference that keeps two branches from filing the same tool twice. |
| **Use case** | What it is for, in service terms. |
| **Data involved** | What it touches. Flag patron data, PII, circulation or search records explicitly. |
| **Service owner** | The named person accountable for this entry. Not a department. A person. |
| **Approval status** | Approved, conditional, pilot, declined, retired. |
| **Risk tier** | Low (internal, no patron data) or full review (patron-facing or data-touching). See impact review below. |
| **Stewardship review** | The environmental and labor review from the evaluation checklist: complete, not applicable, or a link to the assessment. The registry records that the question was asked; the completed checklist holds the answers. |
| **Conditions of use** | The terms the approval depends on. |
| **Provenance of entry** | How the library learned this tool existed: which trigger, who raised it, when. This is what makes the registry auditable rather than merely current. |
| **Review date** | When this entry is next looked at. Not optional. An entry with no review date is decaying. |
| **Change log** | Dated record of every change to this entry and who made it. This is the custody chain. |
| **Superseded by** | If a version or replacement takes over, point to it here. Authority records track when a heading is replaced; so does this. |

## Impact review

Low-risk internal tools get the entry above and light guidance. Patron-facing or data-touching tools get a fuller review before approval. Run that review on four questions. They are the four operational meanings of trust, and they make "risk tier" into something a manager can actually assess instead of a label.

- **Accuracy.** Does the tool do what the vendor claims it does? On what evidence? Who verified it, not the vendor.
- **Autonomy.** Does it decide or recommend? Is a person in the loop before its output reaches a patron, or after, or never?
- **Recourse.** When the tool is wrong, can a patron or a staff member contest it and get it corrected? Through what path?
- **Enforceability.** Can the library actually hold the vendor to the stated terms? Is the data handling in the contract or only in the marketing? What happens to patron data if the vendor is acquired or the service ends?

A tool that cannot answer enforceability is not low-risk just because it is convenient.

## Minimum viable governance

This template scales down without losing the custody chain. For a small or under-resourced library, the minimum is not a policy binder. It is one gate.

- The registry is one shared document.
- The maintaining authority is one named person who has to sign each entry.
- The impact review for a patron-facing tool is the four questions above, answered in a paragraph.
- The single rule that makes it real: procurement and renewal cannot bypass the entry.

That is governance encoded into the one process where the decision actually happens, rather than written into a policy that is filed and never maintained. The binder is compliance as prose, and prose is where under-resourced libraries fail, because no one has the time to keep the prose alive. The gate is compliance as code. It scales down to one person and one list and still answers the only question that matters.

---

## What this template does not do

It does not assess organizational readiness, write staff guidance, design public AI literacy programming, or build the transparent-reporting cadence. Those are the other responsibilities of AI stewardship, and they travel with this one. The registry is the spine. It is not the whole body.
