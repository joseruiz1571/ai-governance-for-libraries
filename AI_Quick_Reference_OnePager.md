# AI Quick Reference

## For staff and volunteers at [Library Name]

*Print this. Post it at the service desk and in the staff workroom. When in doubt, ask before proceeding.*

---

### You MAY use AI tools freely for

- Drafting and editing newsletters, social media posts, and announcements
- Brainstorming program ideas, instructional outlines, and booklist themes
- Researching topics, background, and context for programs and displays
- Proofreading and grammar-checking written communications
- Creating administrative templates with aggregate or de-identified data
- Using embedded AI features in already-approved library software

### You MUST ask the director (or designated committee) first before

- Using any patron data in a closed AI system
- Activating new AI features in the ILS, catalog, website, or a database
- Publishing AI-assisted content externally in the library's name
- Using AI to select, weed, or rank materials for the collection
- Buying or subscribing to a new AI tool for library use

### You must NEVER

- Enter confidential patron records, borrowing or search history, or reference questions into any AI tool
- Enter a patron's personal information, especially a minor's, into an open AI system
- Use AI to screen, rank, or evaluate job or volunteer candidates
- Use AI to profile, score, or predict the behavior of identifiable patrons
- Use AI to generate official library positions or collection decisions without review
- Use AI to create misleading, deceptive, or fabricated content

---

### Always remember

- **You are responsible** for the content you produce, even when AI helped.
- **AI output is a first draft, not a finished product.** Review everything.
- **The key question is always: what information am I putting into this tool?**
- **When in doubt, ask** your supervisor or the director.
- **Be transparent** about your use of AI. Honesty builds trust.
